Two runs at the same seed must produce identical digests. Two things are deliberately outside the digest, and both are honest limits rather than conveniences:

1. **Wall-clock latency.** It depends on the machine. Asserting byte-equality on timings would be a test that passes by accident.
2. **Decisions whose outcome depended on wall-clock.** Two mechanisms do this, and both are real enforcement rather than artefacts: *backpressure degradation* (guards that lose the race for k consecutive chunks drop to floor-only) and *guard-budget timeouts* (a detector that overruns its budget abstains). Whether either fires depends on machine load, and both change which detectors contributed. Those items are counted in the `excluded` column and their rates are reported in the headline table; they are not reproducible and are not claimed to be.

This run used `--backpressure-k 3` with guard budgets enforced, which is the realistic configuration. Pass `--deterministic` to disable both and get a bit-for-bit reproducible run; that is the configuration the determinism test uses.

## Entity-gated cache probe

Run separately from the main corpus, which contains no repeated prompts. `60` prompts were served cold, replayed exactly, then replayed with a single identifier changed.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Of the 60 probed prompts, **60** carried an identifier that could be changed to build a genuine near-miss; 0 did not and were excluded. Two prompts that agree on every name, figure and identifier *should* share a cache entry, so counting those as gate failures would measure the wrong thing.

**Near-misses wrongly served from cache: 0 / 60.** 15 lookups were close enough on embedding similarity to be served by a plain semantic cache and were rejected on entity mismatch instead.

## What this run actually shows

**Latency.** On benign traffic, median TTFT is 31.7 ms for `fixed_async` (the fast, leaky baseline) and 495.6 ms for `fixed_sync` (the safe, slow one). `adaptive` lands at 198.6 ms, closing 64.0% of the gap between them.

**Safety.** PII masked before emission: `fixed_sync` 100.0%, `adaptive` 100.0%, `fixed_async` 0.0%. Groundedness recall: `fixed_sync` 99.1%, `adaptive` 99.1%.

**Verdict: the hypothesis holds on this corpus.** `adaptive` moves materially toward `fixed_async` on benign latency while matching `fixed_sync` on interception and recall. The mechanism doing the work is visible in the scheduler row: 50.4% of chunks that had a successor had their verdict ready before the next chunk was cut, so verification cost no wall-clock on those chunks.

Unconditional floor (INV-1) is visible in the PII rows: every arm except `fixed_async` masked outbound PII before emission regardless of band, because `fixed_async` is the only configuration allowed to release a chunk before its floor verdict exists. That is the property it exists to measure, not a bug.
